Chapter three. Remember the picture from chapter two — references pointing at objects on the heap. The mantra for today, say it with me: assignment copies the arrow, never the object. When t1 = t2 runs, Java re-aims one arrow; the old object is instantly orphaned and garbage-collectable. Primitives are different — assignment copies the value itself, so x = 6 never touches y. Keep this diagram in your notes: the aliasing trap is a standard exam code-reading item. Three lines are enough to prove it — let's write them.
