Two operators, two questions. Double-equals asks: same house? equals asks: same floor plan? I say it twice on purpose — it is the model that sticks. Double-equals compares references, the arrows themselves; equals compares contents, the state inside the object. And keep the boundary clean: the relational operators, less-than and friends, only ever compare values — for objects, equals is the tool. This is a standard exam short answer and code-reading item. Java planted two landmines in this exact spot — vote carefully on the next slide.
