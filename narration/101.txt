Two structural facts, and the slide stays light. First: a class may implement many interfaces — Java's answer to multiple inheritance, with no diamond problem because interfaces carry no state and no bodies. Second: an interface can extend another interface, and that arrow is solid — the inheritance arrow — the checklist simply grows longer. One caution for the exam: if two implemented interfaces declare the same method name, one signature satisfies both; conflicts are rare and resolvable, at most one multiple-choice item. The weight of this chapter sits on 9.2, 9.3, and 9.6.
