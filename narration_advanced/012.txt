Three traps, thirty seconds each. One: a class defined inside a method or even a loop is perfectly legal, and outside that block the type does not exist — you can only hand it out as its interface. Two: when you extend an inner class, the hidden outer reference does not come free; the derived constructor must call wi-dot-super to attach an outer instance, and the compiler enforces it. Three: you cannot override an inner class the way you override a method — the two Inner names are just two separate types. Any of these three is fair game as an exam code-reading question.
