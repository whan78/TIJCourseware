Same interface, opposite internals — so the same operations have opposite costs. ArrayList is one contiguous array: indexing is instant, but inserting at the front shuffles half the array. LinkedList is separate nodes with pointers: splicing in the middle is two pointer writes, but reaching element ten-thousand walks ten thousand links. The engineering lesson: declare the variable as List, choose the implementation by the dominant operation of your workload. And if you keep calling contains in a loop, neither will save you — reach for a HashSet instead.
