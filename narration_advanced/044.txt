A first working taste of regex — enough to read, not yet to write complex patterns. Three habits to take away. First, the doubled backslash: the string literal needs \d to deliver \d to the regex engine. Second, split with \W-plus collapses any run of non-word characters into one separator — that is how double spaces stop producing empty tokens. Third, the compile-match loop: compile the pattern once, then find and group repeatedly. We will use regex again in I/O for scanning files, so keep this slide handy.
