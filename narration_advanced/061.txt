Generics scale down to single methods. The Generator interface parameterizes what it produces; CoffeeGen fixes T to Coffee in its implements clause. The fill method is the interesting part: its type parameter appears BEFORE the return type, which marks the method itself as generic — needed for static methods, since they cannot touch the class parameter. Inference does the bookkeeping: T comes from the list argument, and in the map example, from the assignment target. When you later see Collections.emptyMap, you now know the trick that makes it work.
