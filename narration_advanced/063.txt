Erasure is the honest heart of this chapter. Java five generics had to run beside a decade of non-generic code, so the designers erased type parameters in the bytecode: your Holder of T is, to the JVM, a holder of Object, and the compiler inserts the casts at the edges — on every get out of a typed reference. The consequences list is your exam checklist: no new T, no T dot class, no instanceof T, no arrays of T. And the escape hatch is bounds: extending changes the erasure target from Object to the bound, which restores real operations. Wildcards exist precisely because erasure makes generics invariant.
