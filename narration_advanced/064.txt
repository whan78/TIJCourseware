Bounds turn erasure from a limitation into a dial. With a bare T, erasure gives you Object and nothing else — you cannot even call area on your own type parameter. Add extends Shape and two things happen at once: callers may only supply Shapes or subtypes, and inside, T erases to Shape, so t.area compiles. Note the ordering rule in multiple bounds — the class comes first — and the self-bound idiom at the bottom: comparable to its own type, which is exactly how the library's sort declares its element type. Read any JavaDoc generic clause with these two roles in mind.
