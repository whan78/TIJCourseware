Back to basics with precision. An array is an object: created on the heap with new or a literal, carrying a final length field — square brackets, not parentheses, which is why the exam loves mixing it with String dot length. Identity semantics matter: two identical-content arrays are still different objects, so plain equals is reference equality and Arrays.equals does the element comparison. And note the zero-initialization guarantee — fresh arrays hold zeros, falses, and nulls, never garbage, which is a genuine safety difference from C.
