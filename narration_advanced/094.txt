The two idioms you will type for the rest of your career. Reading: wrap the file in a reader, buffer it, loop on readLine until null — note the StringBuilder assembly from chapter thirteen. Writing: FileWriter to BufferedWriter to PrintWriter, then println for everything. The safety point deserves its own line: buffers hold data until flush or close, so a program that forgets close can silently lose output — the try-with-resources form from chapter twelve removes the risk by construction. Exceptions here are the checked IOException family, which is why every method in sight declares throws.
