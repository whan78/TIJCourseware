A first look at new I/O, three minutes of concept. Old streams move a byte per call; NIO moves blocks between a channel — the door to a file or socket — and a buffer, a fixed block with three cursors: position, limit, capacity. The dance is always the same: read fills, flip pivots the buffer from writing mode to reading mode by swapping its cursors, then you drain it. Direct buffers can even sit in native memory for zero-copy transfers. You will not hand-code this daily, but servers and file copies beneath every framework run exactly this loop — worth recognizing on sight.
