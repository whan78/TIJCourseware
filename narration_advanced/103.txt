Here enums stop being constants and become classes with data. Each constant line passes arguments to a constructor — description strings here — and you add methods like any class, including your own toString. The constructor is implicitly private: the JVM builds the constants once, and nobody — including you — can add a fifth witch. That closed-instance property is what makes switch exhaustive and what makes the enum safe to compare with ==. Design takeaway: whenever you see int constants plus a comment explaining them, this slide is the upgrade.
