Three built-ins, each changing compiler behavior rather than program behavior. Override is the safety net that every codebase should mandate: annotate an intended override and any typo in name or parameters becomes a compile error instead of a silently-new method. Deprecated stamps a warning onto every caller, guiding migration before removal. SuppressWarnings is the pressure valve — legal, but professional style keeps it scoped to the exact statement with a comment saying why. Note that none of these three is read at run time by reflection — they are compile-time contracts.
