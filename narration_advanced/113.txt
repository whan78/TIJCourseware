Where annotations pay off: tools read them. Combine chapter fourteen's reflection with runtime retention and you get a program that discovers annotated methods without knowing their names — isAnnotationPresent to filter, getAnnotation to unpack the values, invoke to run. That loop, plus reporting, is essentially JUnit. Point at the retention policy: change it to class and this program goes blind — retention is the contract between the annotation author and every future tool. Now when Spring says it scans your components, you know the exact mechanism.
