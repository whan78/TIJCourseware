The villain of the chapter, drawn frame by frame. Increment looks atomic but is three steps — read, add, write. Interleave two threads so both read five before either writes six, and one increment silently disappears. The horror is the diagnostics: the program is wrong only SOMETIMES, and differently on different runs — with a thousand threads your counter lands below a thousand at an unpredictable value. Any read-modify-write or check-then-act on shared data is a candidate. Three guards exist — synchronized, explicit Lock, and atomic classes — and choosing among them is the exam-worthy skill this chapter trains.
